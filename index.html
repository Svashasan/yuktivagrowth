Cost per qualified lead down 38%

B2B SaaS, 40-person team. Paid search. Five months, 2025.

Situation and objective

A project-management tool for construction firms was spending steadily on Google Ads but sales rejected most of the leads as the wrong company size. The objective was to cut the cost of a lead sales would accept, without cutting the budget.

Starting point

Cost per qualified lead averaged 340 across the five months before the engagement. Campaigns were optimised to form submissions, so bidding rewarded cheap, unqualified traffic.

Strategy and prioritisation

Three things in order: get qualified-lead data back into the ad platform, rebuild campaigns around company-size intent, then fix the landing pages. Nothing else until those were done.

Execution and notable decisions

Imported CRM qualification status as an offline conversion within the first three weeks. Paused twelve campaigns that had never produced an accepted lead, which was uncomfortable for the client because they drove most of the form fills. Built 14 landing pages by job role instead of by feature.

Results, timeframe and measurement

Cost per qualified lead fell to 211 by month five, a 38% drop against the five-month baseline on a flat budget. Measured in the client's CRM, not the ad platform. Lead volume dropped 20% in month one before recovering; this is normal when you stop paying for unqualified clicks.

Lessons and next steps

The offline conversion import did more than any copy change. Next step is extending the same qualification feedback loop to LinkedIn Ads.

Non-branded organic clicks up 2.4×

Professional services firm. SEO and content. Nine months, 2024–25.

Situation and objective

A 60-person accounting and advisory firm had steady traffic that was almost entirely people searching for its name. The objective was to reach prospects who did not know the firm yet.

Starting point

Search Console showed 1,900 non-branded clicks a month, and roughly 300 service pages were either not indexed or indexed as near-duplicates.

Strategy and prioritisation

Fix what stopped existing pages from ranking before writing anything new. Then target the questions buyers ask in the months before they switch advisers.

Execution and notable decisions

Consolidated the duplicate service pages to 22 rewritten ones, fixed canonical tags and internal links, and briefed 14 articles that the firm's in-house writer produced. I reviewed every draft against the keyword map before publication.

Results, timeframe and measurement

Non-branded clicks reached 4,600 a month after nine months, 2.4 times the baseline, measured in Search Console over the same calendar months. Enquiries from organic search are tracked in the firm's CRM and rose in step, though the firm's referral programme ran at the same time, so I don't attribute all of the enquiry growth to this work.

Lessons and next steps

The technical consolidation produced most of the gain in the first three months. The content programme continues at two articles a month.

Trial-to-paid conversion up 27%

Online education platform. Lifecycle email. Twelve weeks, 2025.

Situation and objective

A platform selling professional certification courses had a fourteen-day free trial and one weekly newsletter that went to everyone. The objective was to convert more trials to paid subscriptions.

Starting point

Trial-to-paid conversion for the previous quarter's cohort was 11%, measured in the billing system. Most people who did not convert had never completed a lesson.

Strategy and prioritisation

Treat the first completed lesson as the activation moment and build the programme around getting people there.

Execution and notable decisions

Built three journeys in the client's platform: an onboarding sequence triggered on sign-up, a nudge sequence for anyone with no lesson completed by day three, and a trial-expiry sequence with a time-limited offer. The client's engineers supplied the product events; I wrote and segmented everything else.

Results, timeframe and measurement

Conversion for the twelve-week cohort reached 14%, a 27% relative increase on the prior cohort, measured in billing. Pricing did not change during the period. A small discount in the expiry sequence contributed; without it the lift would likely have been lower.

Lessons and next steps

The nudge sequence outperformed the onboarding sequence. Next step is a win-back journey for lapsed subscribers.
